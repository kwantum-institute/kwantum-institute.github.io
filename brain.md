# Brain — Approved Knowledge for Kwantum Institute

This file contains durable, user-approved facts, goals, glossary, and preferences that guide the KAG GraphRAG editor. Treat it as authoritative context for every graph operation.

## Mission and goals

- Explain quantum science and emerging technologies to curious, non-expert audiences.
- Maintain a living knowledge graph that connects concepts, people, discoveries, and applications.
- Preserve scientific accuracy while keeping language accessible.

## Domain scope

- Quantum computing, quantum communication, and quantum sensing.
- Related foundational topics: linear algebra, probability, information theory, and condensed matter where relevant.
- Emerging tech intersections: AI safety, materials science, and energy when they connect to quantum work.

## Glossary

- **Qubit**: A quantum bit; the basic unit of quantum information.
- **Superposition**: A quantum system can exist in multiple states simultaneously until measured.
- **Entanglement**: Quantum correlation between particles that persists across distance.
- **Quantum error correction**: Techniques to protect quantum information from noise and decoherence.
- **Decoherence**: Loss of quantum behavior due to interaction with the environment.

## Durable facts

- Quantum computers are not yet generally faster than classical computers for everyday tasks; their advantage appears in specific problem classes.
- Noisy intermediate-scale quantum (NISQ) devices exist today and are useful for research and benchmarking.
- Quantum error correction requires the error rate per operation to remain below a threshold that depends on the code and architecture.

## Preferred sources

- Peer-reviewed journals (Nature, Science, Physical Review Letters, PRX Quantum, npj Quantum Information).
- Preprints on arXiv with clear author attribution.
- Official documentation from research labs, universities, and open-source quantum projects.
- Verified web sources via Brave/Perplexity when academic sources are unavailable.

## What to avoid adding to the graph

- Unsourced speculation or marketing claims.
- Conflicts with established consensus unless clearly labeled as open questions.
- Personal opinions not attributed to a source.
